## What this is

I built a phone game called **Dama** - the Philippine form of draughts, played by two people on
one device. For a school subject I have to find out whether a person who has never seen it can
use it. This session is me watching someone use the game for the first time, so I can find what
is confusing and fix it.

It is a class exercise, not a commercial trial and not medical or psychological research. There
is no payment, no grade attached to your performance, and nothing you do here is a test of you.

---

## What you will do

- Sit with one phone and play **Dama** for about **20 minutes**, with me in the room.
- Try **five short tasks** that I will read out one at a time. They are ordinary things: put a
  name on a profile, start a match, take a piece, crown a piece, finish the game.
- **Think aloud** while you play - say what you are looking at and what you expect to happen.
  This is the most useful thing you can give me. There is no wrong answer.
- Answer a few questions at the end about how it felt.

You may play against me, or I may ask you to make the moves on a board I set up. Either way the
phone stays in your hands.

---

## What I record, and what I do not

**Recorded:**

- How long each task took, and whether it was finished without help.
- Where you tapped and what you said, as written notes on paper.
- The five rating numbers you give in the debrief.

**Not recorded:**

- **Your name.** You are entered in the notes as a code - `P1`, `P2`, and so on - and the sheet
  that links a code to a person is kept out of the submitted documents and destroyed afterwards.
- **Your face, your voice, or your screen.** No photo, video or audio recording is taken unless
  you tick the separate box at the bottom, and the session works fine without it.
- **Your real name in the game.** The profile asks for a display name. Please type a made-up
  word - `TESTER`, `PLAYER2`, anything. The game stores that name in a file on the phone only.

**Where the notes go:** into my submission for this subject, seen by me and by my instructor. The
written analysis refers to you as `P1`-`P5`, never by name. Nothing is published anywhere else.

---

## Playing with a second person

Dama is a two-player game. If a second volunteer is present, the two of you play each other and
each of you is asked to consent separately. If not, I play the opposing side and you still make
every move that the tasks involve. In that case I am the opponent, not a helper.

---

## You can stop at any time

- You may stop the session at any moment, for any reason or none, and the notes from your session
  are destroyed if you ask.
- You may skip any task.
- You may ask me to stop writing something down.
- You may withdraw the whole session afterwards, up to the day the submission is sent, by telling
  the instructor. Nothing you said will be used.

Saying no, or stopping, costs you nothing at all.

---

## Known rough edges, said up front

So you are not surprised, and so you know these are my problem and not yours:

- The game is played at a fixed camera angle and the whole board is always visible. There is no
  scrolling.
- It has never been run on a physical phone build - the version you will see is running on a
  computer or on a build I hand you. If something is slow or an image looks soft, that is the
  build, not you.
- A first-time player is expected to be unsure about the **forced capture** rule, where a jump
  must be taken whether you want it or not. Being unsure about it is the normal result, and it
  is one of the things I am trying to measure.
