1. The defect that taught me the most

TC-FLOW-06, the route back to the title screen. I carried that finding forward from Week 14 as M2 and thought I already understood it. I understood the symptom. What the test taught me was the size of the cause. The case does not ask whether a MENU button exists; it asks whether the game can be left. The baseline measured value was PausePanel/MenuButton present=False, DamaBoard.ReturnToMenu present=False, and the second half is the part that mattered: there was no method to call. DamaBoard had no transition from Paused or GameOver back to Ready. So this was not a missing button, it was a missing direction in the game's own model, and the pause panel had been offering RESTART as its only exit because there was nothing else to offer.

That reframed the other three findings. Each was the game keeping a fact and never carrying it to the screen: the end screen said only RED WIN! because DeclareWinner received the winner and the reason derived in CheckForWin was thrown away; the smallest caption anywhere was 14 px because each panel builder picked its own number and nothing owned "the smallest text". Three defects, one habit.

2. Reproducing and correcting it

Reproducing took two taps, written down: START, then PAUSE, and the panel offers RESUME, RESTART and SOUND and nothing else. Making it reproducible for someone else became TC-FLOW-06 in Assets/Scripts/Editor/Week15Checks.cs - the same file produced the failing line above and the passing line after the fix. The correction had two halves that had to agree:

  • Week15Fixes.EnsureButton builds PausePanel/MenuButton beside RestartButtonPause and WinPanel/MenuButtonWin beside RestartButtonWin: same anchor line, 340 px right, 320 x 96. It went beside RESTART and not under it because the arithmetic refused it - four 96 px controls plus three 12 px gaps need 420 px and the pause title leaves a 357 px band. Stacked, I would have shipped a second defect while fixing the first.
  • DamaBoard.ReturnToMenu restores Time.timeScale = 1 before it shows the title screen. Both screens are reached with the clock stopped, so without that line the title screen is drawn and dead to the touch, and a screenshot would not have told me. The case asserts it: StartPanel=True PausePanel=False HUDPanel=False timeScale=1.00.

The retest is the same case, the same assertion, against the corrected build. Nothing about the test moved - only the code under it - which is the only reason I trust the result.

3. The regression risk I discovered

Two, and I found neither by reasoning.

The contrast margins got thinner because of a fix. Raising five captions from 14 px to 22 px touches no colour, so I assumed contrast was untouched. The delta file says otherwise: PausePanel/SoundButton/Text fell from 9.29:1 to 6.59:1, because lightening a fill to give the control a visible edge spends the contrast of the white label on it. The edge needs 3.0:1 against the panel and the label needs 4.5:1 against the fill, and those pull against each other. The pass is bounded on both sides and the label wins, but three controls now sit at 3.16:1, 3.17:1 and 3.19:1 against a 3.0 floor and ProfilePanel/SideButton sits at 3.03:1. Nothing warns me if the next edit pushes them under. The audit runs before every submission from now on.

The screenshots were lying, and only the numbers caught it. My first capture run drew the board from the 24 opening pieces while the grid held a seeded end position, so E2_end_screen.png showed a HUD reading Red: 12 Blue: 12 beside a summary reading RED 1-1. Both were rendered correctly from two different sources of truth: seeding by reflection changes what the game believes, not what is drawn. The harness now rebuilds the visible pieces before every capture, and the Rebuilt N visible piece(s) lines in week15_shots.txt are what let a reviewer check the counts in a picture against the state it claims. I left that disclosure in the transcript rather than re-taking the images quietly, because a screenshot that agrees with the log by accident is worse than one that documents why it once did not.
