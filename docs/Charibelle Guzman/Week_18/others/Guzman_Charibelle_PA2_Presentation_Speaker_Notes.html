PA2 — Speaker Notes

Charibelle Guzman · BSIT-4 · MOBILE DEV 002, Module 5 · Week 18

These are the notes that go with Guzman_Charibelle_PA2_Presentation — what to say on each slide and which file to open if a question follows. They are notes, not a script, and they deliberately contain no prepared answers: the questions in Evidence/defense_prep.md are the ones to be answered live.

Slide 1 — Title. Fifteen seconds. Name, course, week, and the one-line pitch: draughts as it is played here, two people, one phone. Say the tag v1.0.0-pa2 out loud so the submission is identifiable.

Slide 2 — Why this game. Explain that the theme is carried by the rules and not by a texture: compulsory capture, chaining, the flying Dama are what make it Dama. If asked where the rules live, open Assets/Scripts/DamaBoard.cs and point at the move generators.

Slide 3 — Target player. The important sentence is that the player is two people sharing one device. Everything else on this slide is a consequence: the 96 canvas-pixel touch floor, the top-down board, the local record. If asked for the derivation of 96: 48 dp at xxhdpi is 144 physical px, and 144 / 1.50 with the project's 1280x720 scaler is 96 canvas px.

Slide 4 — Core loop. Walk the loop on the deck, then say it is three taps from a finished match to a new one. Mention that MENU on the pause panel exists because of a defect found in Week 15, not because it was planned — the defect log is Week_15/Evidence/defect_log.md.

Slide 5 — Rules. The key engineering point: these are constraints on generation, not checks after the fact. GetCaptureMoves is consulted first, and if it returns anything the simple moves are never offered — which is also why tapping a man with no legal move does nothing.

Slide 6 — Onboarding, credits, exit. Describe the three panels as a module requirement that was missing until Week 17. EXIT is worth a sentence: the wire is proven by a test that names the handler, the Android behaviour is not proven, because no build ran.

Slide 7 — Architecture. Draw the boundary out loud: DamaBoard decides what happened, ProfilePanel decides what is shown, PlayerProfile decides what is stored. If asked why the UI is built in code, the answer is reproducibility — and be honest that it has a cost: a builder that runs twice can duplicate what it builds, which is exactly DEF-17-05.

Slide 8 — Player data. Say both halves: what the signature does (tamper-evident, atomic write, clamped fields, rejected-file recovery) and what it does not (encryption, or any defence against the device's owner). Then the path: persistentDataPath is built from productName and companyName, so the version went into bundleVersion instead.

Slide 9 — Demonstration. Point at the nine files and at Evidence/demo_rehearsal.txt. Mention the hash gate and why it exists: the first Week 17 run wrote six names over four pictures because the Editor window was not foreground. Say plainly that the run is an Editor run.

Slide 10 — Testing. Give the three counts: 27 / 0 / 1, 5 / 0 / 0, 148 / 0 / 0 / 4. Then the sentence that matters: TC-COMPAT-01 is blocked on hardware and is not counted as a pass anywhere in the package.

Slide 11 — Profiling. Lead with the method: measure, change one thing, measure again. The two changes were allocation-free move generation and piece pooling. Do not claim a frame-time win — the frame was already inside budget and the mean moved with the session. The evidence is Week_16/Evidence/metrics.md.

Slide 12 — Usability and accessibility. The audit is a floor. Name the thin margins (3.16–3.19:1) and the missing pieces (dynamic type, screen reader) before anyone asks. Say that no human playtest was run, and that the Week 14 kit is in Week_14/Playtest/ unused.

Slide 13 — Limitations. This is the slide to be slow on. No APK, no handset, Editor numbers only, haptics unproven, EXIT unfired, caption reads · Editor, defense not in the package. Each one is a row in the README's known-limitations table and in Evidence/final_pre_submission_checklist.md.

Slide 14 — Next. Close with the one gap that matters most: an installable build on a handset. Then the playtest, then dynamic type. End on the rule that produced the whole method: measure before claiming.

If there is time left. Open the three artefacts that carry the submission: Evidence/final_results.md (the gates), Evidence/demo_rehearsal.txt (the nine captures with hashes), and Evidence/repo_state.txt (the tag and commit). Those three answer most questions about what was actually done.
